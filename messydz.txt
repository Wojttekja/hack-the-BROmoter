  1. The judge cache is poisoned by id reuse (this is the big one)

  Judge._cache is keyed by (id_a, id_b) — judge.py:113. But breed() regenerates
  ids every generation: the elites are always named elite_01…elite_20, and their
  children are always elite_03_007 etc. So the same id means a different 
  sequence each generation.

  ids reused for DIFFERENT sequences: 314 of 947
  elite_02  gen 0: AATGAAAGATGAGCTAAGAGATTATGGATA…
            gen 1: TGTTGTATTTTTTTGTTCTATGGAGCTTAA…   ← same id, different seq

  main.py:203 does judge.sequences.update(...), which remaps id→sequence, but
  the cache keeps the old verdict. From generation 2 onward, essentially the
  entire top of every tournament — the elite_* pairs, exactly the comparisons
  that decide who survives — is served from stale verdicts about sequences that
  no longer exist. Fix: key the cache on the sequence strings (or a hash), not 
  the id. One-line change, and it also makes the cache work across generations,
  which is what you want.

  2. 94% of your comparisons are ties, so the ranking is input order

  gen 0: 1398 calls | 492 ties
  gen 1: 1400 calls | 622 ties
  gen 2: 1396 calls | 655 ties   → 655/698 pairs = 94% ties, and rising

  The population has converged into a blob the Judge cannot resolve. In
  bucket_sort_sequences, a tie gives both sides 0.5, so nearly everyone ends the
  Swiss with score ≈ rounds/2, and sorted(ids, key=lambda i: -score[i]) is a
  stable sort — ties keep input order, and the input order is pool = candidates
  with the elites first. The elites therefore re-elect themselves every
  generation regardless of merit.

  That, plus finding 1, fully explains points 18.0 on every single submission
  across 11 generations.

  What to actually add

  a) Anchor ladder instead of within-population Swiss. This is the highest-value
  change. The Judge can't separate neighbours, but it can separate distant
  sequences. Fix a ladder of ~10 reference sequences spanning weak→strong (wild
  type, a few weak naturals from Promotory.csv, your best-so-far), and score
  each candidate by how far up the ladder it beats. 20 calls/candidate → at full
  rate that's ~120 candidates/min. This gives you (i) a real scalar with
  resolution, (ii) scores comparable across generations, which Swiss never gives
  you, and (iii) it subsumes what your friends get from re-ingesting wildtype,
  plus a number.

  b) Fit Bradley–Terry over the whole comparison history. You've thrown away
  thousands of verdicts. Keep every (winner, loser, tie) in a table, fit a
  latent strength per sequence by logistic regression, ties as half-weight. Uses
  all evidence instead of one generation's worth, and gives calibrated
  uncertainty so you know which candidates are worth spending more calls on.

  c) Split the two objectives — you're 1st in TOP100 and 3rd in TOP10. These
  reward different files. TOP100 wants a uniformly-good 100; TOP10 wants 10
  outliers. A moonshot only has to beat your current 10th best to lift TOP10, so
  the risk is bounded and measurable: breed a high-variance reservoir
  (level-0/L1 latent jumps, mutations=(10,40)), score it against the ladder, and
  admit only the ones that clear your #10. Keep the other ~85 slots as the
  consolidated best.

  d) Escape the local optimum — your min_distance=8 is far too tight. Median
  pairwise distance in the current population is 65, so a threshold of 8 filters
  nothing; all 20 "diverse" parents are one lineage. Run an island model: 4–6
  sub-populations seeded from different species in Promotory.csv (you have 19),
  evolved independently with occasional migration. That's both your TOP10 upside
  and your hedge against the blob.

  e) Stop guessing what the model likes — measure it. ~20 judge calls per
  experiment, and it's the best slide you'll have for the jury (50% of the
  grade):
  - GC-content sweep; homopolymer runs; does duplicating a 20 bp block help?
  - Insert a consensus TATA at varying distances from the TSS — where does the
    model want it?
  - Does the model reward N? You're allowed 10%. Ns feed 0.25 to all four
    channels. Probably penalised, but it's 20 calls to find out whether it's an
    exploit or a trap.
  - Cluster the high-wagaP positions from /nawigator/mapa — contiguous
    high-weight blocks are the model's implied binding sites. Then
    saturation-scan them (all 3 alternatives at each position, scored on the
    ladder) instead of random point mutation. That converts your random walk
    into actual gradient ascent.

  f) Biological framing to match your friends' filters. Rather than hard
  filters, make them operators: tandem-duplicate putative UAS/activator sites (a
  well-established way to strengthen fungal promoters), keep a pyrimidine-rich
  block near the TSS, remove upstream ATGs/uORFs, avoid cryptic poly-A signals.
  For pks1 under biotic antagonism, light-response (BLR1/2) and stress/cAMP-PKA
  regulated elements are the ones to name when the jury asks.
